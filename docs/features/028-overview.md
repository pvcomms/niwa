# 028 — The overview

## Why

The ask, in the reader's words: the overview effect; attach to a possible future trajectory
that is positive; present short-term discomfort as short-term, against the long-term
potential — feeling good, success — and visualise it. The mechanism they gave: show the life
you could have if you took this uncomfortable step and everything worked out; a person forms
an identity-level affiliation with it and sees it as something they could have; so not taking
the step feels like losing that outcome, even though it is a visualisation; the discomfort,
the friction and the shame of losing it make the step more likely now. And the one line
repeated: the level of discomfort expressed as a function of time and set against the
long-term benefit.

The borrowed move is Frank White's overview effect (1987): seen from far enough out, the thing
that filled the view becomes a small part of a larger whole. Time is where it applies here.
Up close, a step's discomfort is all there is; pulled back through the scales of time, with
the frame kept true to scale, the discomfort keeps its depth and loses its width. The research
under the rest: seeing an act from farther off — why rather than how — strengthens
self-control and the choice of later, larger goods (Fujita, Trope, Liberman & Levin-Sagi
2006); a future self felt as the same person makes costs borne for them easier now
(Hershfield 2011); losses weigh more than gains of the same size (Kahneman & Tversky 1979), so
the difference between the two roads is drawn and named as what not taking it gives up. Two
findings shape it against the naive version. The outcome imagined alone drains the effort it
should summon (Kappes & Oettingen 2011), so the dip is always drawn beside the life, never the
life alone — which makes the drawing a mental contrast by construction. And bad feelings are
forecast as stronger and longer than they turn out (Wilson & Gilbert 2005), so a step taken
keeps what the reader said before beside what it was after; that record is what can correct
the next forecast. Shame is left out on purpose: it turns people away from what they are
ashamed of, where guilt about an act turns them toward repair (Tangney & Dearing 2002). The
loss and the friction are built; no line on the sheet shames.

## What changes

- **A twentieth view, `/overview`, as one sheet.** The step in a line, in the display face.
  Under it the drawing, large: a time axis from a little before now, the level of now drawn
  across, and two roads forking at now — the road without the step, dashed, and the road with
  it, inked: a dip below now for as long as the discomfort lasts, then a rise through the
  reader's marks. The dip is filled in the alarm's red, the difference between the roads in
  the course's green.
- **The altitude.** The drawing opens close — the frame two and a half times the dip, so the
  dip is two fifths of it — and after a breath pulls back on its own, the frame multiplying at
  an even pace in log time out to the horizon, the way the Eameses' camera rose in _Powers of
  Ten_. At every altitude the frame is linear and to scale. A caption says what is in it and
  how much of it the dip is: _the next 75 minutes · the 30 minutes are 40% of it_ … _the next
  five years · the 30 minutes are 1 part in 87,700 of it_. At full distance the dip is a
  hairline at now that has kept its depth, labelled on a leader: _7 of 10 for 30 minutes_. A
  rail under the drawing is the altitude, drawn in the hand with the scales it passes (_the
  dip · a day · a week · a month · a year · five years_) and a ring where the frame is: drag
  it, click it, arrow it, or _pull back_ / _come in close_; space does the same, and ⌘ with the
  wheel (or a pinch) over the drawing goes by hand. Reduced motion jumps instead of flying.
- **Every number is the reader's, typed or dragged.** How hard at its worst (1–10) — drag the
  bottom of the dip or type it. How long (minutes, hours or days). How far out the life is set
  (three months, a year, two, five or ten years). How far above now the road is by a week, a
  month, a year (and five years, where the horizon is ten), and by the horizon — drag the
  marks or type them. Where the road without it is by the horizon (−10 to 10; 0, the default,
  is as it is now). The rise passes through every mark, monotone between them in log time, so
  it never overshoots what was marked.
- **The life, and who you are on that road.** The life if the step is taken and it goes as
  hoped, as it is, in the first person; who the reader is on that road, in a line (_I am
  someone who asks._), written large at the horizon's end of the drawing; what is true by each
  mark, written at the mark; what the road without it is like. While the horizon is still out
  past the frame, a note at the right edge points to it: _five years out · 7 above now →_.
- **Take the step.** The clock starts and is kept with the step; the drawing comes close and
  a line in the accent moves through the dip as the minutes pass (_in it · 12 minutes in_).
  _It is over_ stops it; then _at its worst it was_ (1–10) and _it lasted_ (minutes, from the
  clock, editable), beside what was said before, and _afterwards_ in a line. The drawing then
  carries both dips: what was said, dashed, and what it was, filled. _Not yet — stop the
  clock_ takes a start back.
- **Not today is a hold.** Pressing it does nothing; holding it for 2.4 seconds lets the step
  go for today, and while it is held the road with the step and the life fade, evenly, to a
  tenth; let go early and they come straight back. The dip does not fade. After, a line: _let
  go for today · what stood in the way?_ Kept as a day with its reason; one a day.
- **The reading**, facts only: _the dip: 7 of 10 at its worst, for 30 minutes, as you said
  before · 30 minutes is 1 part in 87,700 of five years · as marked, the road with the step is
  5 above now by a year and 7 above now by five years; the road without it stays as it is now
  · as marked, what the road with the step has over the other, across five years, is 147,000
  times the dip · the life: 3 sentences, each written as it is · who you are on that road, in
  your words: I am someone who asks. · put down 27 Sep · let go for today 1 time: 27 Sep_.
  Sentences of the life that still look ahead are counted with the way's rule.
- **The record**: _1 step put down · 1 taken · 0 in it · 0 open · let go for today 1 time,
  across 1 step · at the worst, before and after: 7 → 4 — less than said in 1 of 1 · for how
  long, before and after: 30 minutes → a minute — shorter than said in 1 of 1_; every step as a
  row, taken ones with _said 7 for 30 minutes · was 4 for a minute_. Pairs and counts, never
  an accuracy.
- **Kept as files.** One markdown file per step in `niwa-vault/content/overview/`
  (`NIWA_OVERVIEW_DIR`): the step, the life, who I am on that road, without it, not today (a
  bullet per day with its reason) and afterwards as sections; how hard, how long, the horizon,
  the marks, where the road without it ends, when it was taken and when it was over, how it was
  after, in the frontmatter. The marks are written by name (`when: "a year"`) so the file reads.
- **An example on an empty sheet.** Before anything is put down the sheet draws an example step
  so the instrument is on the table before a word is typed; nothing is kept until the reader
  writes over it and puts it down, takes it, or lets it go for today.
- **The specimen.** Under `NIWA_MODE` the route serves Specimen A's two steps read-only: telling
  the father the month (the move its way left proposed), let go twice and open; the letter to
  the two retainers, taken, with before and after.
- **No model.** Every number and word is the reader's.
- **The Mac app** gets ⌘E.

## What it deliberately does not do

It does not say whether a step is worth it, rank steps, or say the reader should take one. It
does not write the life for them or propose a trajectory: the rise is the reader's marks and
nothing else, and where the curve runs between marks is drawn, never read as a finding — an
early draft read out the day the dip is "made up", and that day came from the curve's shape
between now and the first mark, not from anything marked, so it was taken out. It does not
shame: not today is kept as a day with a reason, and nothing on the sheet says anything about
the reader. It does not turn before and after into an accuracy.

## Notes

The dev server runs React in strict mode, which mounts a view twice. A view that syncs its
address on mount takes `?slug=` off before the second mount reads it; the overview syncs only
once its steps are read. The break (027) syncs the same way and may lose its `?slug=` on reload
for the same reason; it was not changed here.
