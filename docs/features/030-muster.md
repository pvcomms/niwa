# 030 — The muster

## Why

The ask, in the reader's words: Amanda Askell's soldier, scout, paladin and pacifist, "in the
sense of priors + evidence + rationality + decision making + group rationality", built as a
feature — "dynamic".

The four are Askell's (Paladin, pacifist, soldier, scout, 2022). Julia Galef (The Scout
Mindset, 2021) splits reasoning into the soldier's, which defends a position, and the scout's,
which wants to see what is there. Askell's point is that the soldier runs two things together:
being moved by what you would like to be so, and being combative — treating an argument as a
fight, looking for evidence to fortify a position and countering the other side's. They come
apart, and crossed they make four: the soldier (after its side, and fights), the paladin (after
what is so, and fights), the pacifist (after its side, and keeps the peace) and the scout (after
what is so, and keeps the peace). Two of her claims shape the instrument. Combative reasoning is
not the fault: a world of soldiers may do better than a world of pacifists, because soldiers
engage and can be persuaded where pacifists sit on islands nobody reaches — provided the truth
has some advantage in argument and the room is at least a little attuned to it, a court more
than a stage. And group rationality is not individual rationality summed: a paladin's appetite
for being right where others were not sends it to defend positions that are individually unwise
and collectively useful.

The essay does no arithmetic, and the ask names priors, evidence and decisions, so the garden
supplies the formal part and says so on the sheet. A prior is a percent. A piece of evidence is
a way and a weight: how many times more likely it would be seen if the way it points were so.
Being after what is so is moving by a piece's weight whichever way it points; being after your
side is moving by all of what is for it and a quarter of what is against it — the asymmetry
motivated reasoning leaves in updating. Combat is challenging what goes against your side.
Alone with the evidence, then, the scout and the paladin move as one — fighting changes nothing
with no one to fight — and they part only in company. That is the shape of the sheet: the
reader's claim alone with its evidence, then a room.

## What changes

- **A twenty-second view, `/muster`, as one sheet.** The claim in a line, in the display face.
  Before anything came in: where you put it, typed in place, and where that came from — a base
  rate, what I've seen, what I was told, a hunch; which way you would rather it came out — so,
  not so, either way; the line you would act at and what you would do.
- **Alone with the evidence.** A drawing laid out in real pixels on a scale spaced by odds, so a
  piece is a step of its own size wherever it lands: your prior, then every piece in the order
  it came. The scout and the paladin braided in green on your own weights multiplied; the soldier
  solid and the pacifist dashed in ochre, by their rules; your marks in ink, dotted across a piece
  you did not mark; the line you would act at, the ground above it shaded. The prior, the line
  and every mark are dragged in place; the lines draw on from the prior when a claim opens;
  hovering a piece gives each figure's number there. _In another order_ replays the pieces
  shuffled and eases every line to it: the scout and the paladin end where they did, the soldier
  and the pacifist may not. Along the foot each piece is an arrow — up for, down against, as
  long as its weight — crossed in the accent where you argued with it, faint where you let it
  pass.
- **What came in.** Each piece in a line: which way it points; how much — a little (×1.5), some
  (×3), a lot (×8), overwhelming (×30), and a weight written by hand is kept; how you met it —
  argued with it, took it in, let it pass; and where you stood after, or left unsaid.
- **In company: the field.** Askell's quarters drawn by hand — combative up, accuracy-motivated
  to the right — washed by what they are after, the fighting row hatched, each named. A room of
  three of each sits on it as rings that fill as each comes to hold the claim. A click on a
  quarter seats another, a click on a figure sees it out, and the make-up is kept with the claim.
  Hovering a quarter says what its figure does. Your own ring is dragged onto the field — where
  you put yourself on this one, and the only way the sheet ever places you.
- **The room, round by round.** One piece a round to one member, a little or some, most pointing
  at what is so, some fool's gold — and each round said in a line: _a scout was dealt fool's gold
  against the claim (looks like some) · said it to half the room · challenged by a soldier and a
  paladin; shown up by a paladin · struck for everyone_. The dealt piece drops into its holder,
  what is said ripples out, a challenge is drawn across the field, a strike crosses it out, and
  the rings and the beads move once the argument is over. Beside the field, the lanes: each
  quarter's members as beads on a line from not so to so, the line you would act at, the room's
  middle. Run it, a round at a time, scrub it, deal again; suppose it is so or not so; fool's
  gold none, some or a lot; the room as a court — what is shown up is struck for everyone, and
  whoever fought a piece that held takes the whole of it — or a stage, where nothing is ruled.
  The rules are written out under it, every number named.
- **The same deal, five rooms.** This room beside a room of each kind the same size, dealt the
  same pieces seat for seat: each room's middle over the rounds, the shading where most of it
  stands, a cursor at the round.
- **Who else holds a view**, each with where they stand and how you met them — argued it out,
  talked it over, kept it to myself; **afterwards**: _I acted today_, how it came out with its
  day, and afterwards in your words.
- **The reading**, facts only (Specimen A's open claim): _set down 14 Sep · put at 40% before
  anything came in, from what you had seen · you would rather it were so · 5 pieces came in: 3
  for, 2 against · by your weights, 27% · you: 86% · of the weight you gave them, you moved 97%
  on the 3 that went your way and 10% on the 2 that went against you · you argued with 2 of the
  5: 2 of the 2 that went against you, 0 of the 3 that went your way · you would act at 65%: you
  are over it since the 3rd piece; your weights are under it since the 2nd piece · your weights
  would reach your line on a piece weighing 5 to 1 for it · alone with these pieces: the scout
  and the paladin at 27%, the soldier at 80%, the pacifist at 55% · 2 others hold a view, from
  30% to 75%: argued it out with 1, talked it over with 1 · you put yourself among the paladins ·
  it has not come out yet_. With no side you would rather, a line says where the soldier and
  the pacifist could end in another order.
- **The record**: _2 claims mustered · 8 pieces came in, 5 for, 3 against · of the weight you
  gave them, across the record you moved 95% on what went your way and 11% on what went against
  you · you argued with 3 of the 4 that went against you and 0 of the 4 that went your way ·
  came out: 1 (0 so, 1 not so) — The new rates will lose us Harlow: not so, with you at 9% and
  your weights at 23% · where you put yourself: among the paladins 1, among the pacifists 1_;
  every claim as a row.
- **The room's reading**, facts about the model: the round, the room's middle and where most of
  it stands, each quarter's middle, pieces dealt, noticed, said aloud and kept quiet (and of
  those kept quiet, how many pointed at what is so), fool's gold dealt and shown up and by whom,
  when the room's middle reached your line and how many would act.
- **Kept as files.** One markdown file per claim in `niwa-vault/content/muster/`
  (`NIWA_MUSTER_DIR`): the claim, what you'd do, what came in as dated bullets
  (`- 2026-09-17 · against · ×8 · argued with it · then 62 — …`), who else has a view
  (`- Marta · 30 · argued it out`), afterwards; the prior, where it came from, which way you
  would rather, the line, when you acted, how it came out, where you put yourself and the room
  in the frontmatter. A file written by hand with bare dates or bullets that stop after their
  way reads.
- **An example on an empty sheet**: a walk on the ridge on Saturday, the forecast as it came in
  through the week, four pieces, a mark after each. Nothing is kept until it is set down.
- **The specimen.** Under `NIWA_MODE` the route serves Specimen A's two claims read-only — the
  quiet junior and a client account, still open; the new rates and Harlow, come out not so —
  and reads nothing of the garden. The room runs there as anywhere: it is computed in the page.
- **No model.** Every number on the claim is the reader's; the room is a seeded rule.
- **Every stone's** reader and catalogue page carry _muster it_, which opens `/muster?id=` with
  the stone's first sentence as the claim.
- **The Mac app** gets ⌘U.

## What it deliberately does not do

It does not place the reader in a quarter: the ring on the field is theirs to put down, and
the reading only says where they put it. It does not say a move was too large or too small, or
that a way of moving is a fault; the shares are the reader's own numbers side by side. It does
not turn how a claim came out into an accuracy of the reader's judgment — the record keeps
pairs. It does not say what to do at the line. The room says nothing about the reader: it is a
model, and its rules are on the sheet so they can be argued with.

## Out of scope

The reader's own evidence dealt around the room — the room's deal is a fair world by
construction, and the reader's pieces have weights but no truth to be fair to. People the reader
names placed in quarters. A room that learns which of its members to trust. A calibration score
across claims that have come out.

## Notes

The room was tuned against runs, not written to a conclusion. Three hundred deals a setting,
twenty rounds, mean distance from what is so at the end (lower is nearer), fool's gold at some:

| Room of 12    | prior 40, court | prior 50, court | prior 40, stage |
| ------------- | --------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| paladins      | 10.5            | 10.2            | 10.5            |
| scouts        | 29.6            | 25.7            | 29.6            |
| three of each | 34.8            | 26.3            | 47.7            |
| soldiers      | 47.0            | 28.4            | 66.3            |
| pacifists     | 58.2            | 49.1            | 58.2            |

A room of soldiers beats a room of pacifists in a court; on a stage, from a room that starts
leaning the wrong way, it does worse than doing nothing, because each camp keeps its unwelcome
pieces quiet and drops whatever the other side challenges. Scouts and pacifists are the same in a
court and on a stage — neither fights, so no ruling ever reaches them. The first tuning dealt
the room pieces up to ×8 and several a round, and a single strong piece settled a room in one
round; the room is now dealt one piece a round, a little or some. An earlier court bound every
listener to a piece that held, which moved the pacifists as fast as anyone; it now binds only
whoever fought the piece and lost, and the pacifists stay on their islands, as Askell has them.
