# 016 — The way

## Why

The standalone Think Forward-Reverse (`pvcomms/think-forward-reverse`) asks for a goal and a
current state, has a model map a forward path from now and a reverse path back from the
achieved goal, and finds where they converge. It ran on Gemini through a Vercel function,
scored each step's confidence, and kept sessions in the browser.

The ask, in the reader's words: use my own language to write the outcome or ideal reality I
want, and the same language for where I am now; let a model add structure, colour, tangible
ways and timelines from here to there; and put me in a mode where the brain feels it is
already at the destination and acts from there.

The last part is a known technique, not a wish. Imagining an outcome as having already
happened makes its causes easier to name (prospective hindsight — Mitchell, Russo &
Pennington 1989; Klein's pre-mortem is the same move for failure). Writing the then in the
present tense is how the move is made. But dwelling in the outcome alone drains the effort it
is meant to summon (Kappes & Oettingen 2011); what turns the picture into effort is
contrasting it with what in the present stands in the way and giving each obstacle a
when–then plan (Oettingen's mental contrasting; Gollwitzer's implementation intentions). So
the instrument has three parts and a mode: the two texts, the way between them with its
obstacles, and standing at the then to look back. The model proposes; the reader keeps.

## What changes

- **A ninth view, `/way`.** Two papers: _now · where you are_ and _then · as it is_, in the
  reader's own words; the then has a day it is set in. Between them the line from now to
  then: the first moves below it, what had to be true right before above it, and what stands
  in the way underneath, hatched. Kept steps are filled; proposed ones hollow and dashed.
- **The texts read back as facts.** How many of the then's sentences still look ahead
  (_will_, _someday_, _I want_) — said, not corrected. What the then speaks of that the now
  does not, and what the now speaks of that the then has let go: the vocabulary of the
  change, underlined beneath each paper.
- **A model on this machine.** _ask for the way_ sends the two texts, the reader's lanes and
  what is already kept to Ollama at `127.0.0.1:11434` (model `qwen3.6:35b-a3b`, thinking
  off, JSON schema; `NIWA_MODEL` and `NIWA_OLLAMA` override) and gets back four things:
  what changes (each with a lane), what had to be true right before (dated, latest first),
  the first moves (dated), and what stands in the way (each with a when–then). Everything
  arrives **proposed** and hollow; _keep_ makes it the reader's, _drop_ removes it, _keep
  all_ and _drop all_ do both at once. _say the then again_ asks for the then in the first
  person and present tense, specific and sensory, in the reader's words — shown beside the
  original until _take it as mine_ or _drop it_. This is the one call the garden makes to a
  model, it is on this machine, and nothing is written by it. A deployed garden has no model
  and says so.
- **Steps are the reader's to edit**: text, month, direction, kept or not. A kept, dated step
  can be _set down on the line_ — an expected entry on the chronology, tagged `way` — and the
  step remembers which. _+ a first move_, _+ what had to be true_, _+ name one_ (obstacle),
  _+ one_ (change) write without the model.
- **Standing at the then.** `s`, or the chip: the sheet becomes the memoir. _It is Sep 2027.
  It is so._ The then (the model's retelling if kept, else the reader's own), what is
  different now, what had to be true latest first with its months, what nearly stopped it and
  what I did, and the first thing I did. A line under it says to stay a minute and go back to
  the steps.
- **The reading.** _the then is set in Sep 2027 — a year from today · 2 of the then's 4
  sentences still look ahead — will and someday. it is meant to be written as it is · the then
  speaks of window, essay and readers; the now does not · 3 steps yours, 5 proposed and not
  yet yours; 3 dated · the next dated step: … · looking back from the then, what had to be
  true right before: … · 2 in the way, named; 2 with a when–then · 4 things change between the
  now and the then, across place and work._
- **Kept as files.** One markdown file per way at `niwa-vault/content/way/<slug>.md`: the
  then and the now as `## then` / `## now` sections a person can edit in any editor, the
  model's retelling under `## the then, said again`, and the steps, obstacles and changes in
  the frontmatter with `by` (you or proposed) and `kept`. The texts keep themselves a moment
  after typing stops. `NIWA_WAY_DIR` moves the folder.
- **The Mac app** gets ⌘9.

## What it deliberately does not do

It does not score a step's confidence, rank the ways, or say which move is the one. It does
not write the reader's then for them: the retelling is a proposal in their words, kept only
on their say. It does not fetch anything from outside this machine. It does not keep the
reader at the then: the memoir is a mode with a way back, because the research says the
outcome alone saps the effort and the obstacles restore it.

## Out of scope

Streaming the model's answer. Several thens at once (make several ways). Reading the
chronology's present from a way (the chronology is another session's file today). Any model
that is not on this machine.
