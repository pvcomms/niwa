# 032 — The act

## Why

Most of what the garden holds ends in a decision — a call on a fence, a line on an envelope, a place
among values — and about half of the people who mean to act do not (Sheeran 2002). What closes the
gap best in the research is small: the best that would come of it set against what in you would
stand in the way, with a plan for when it does (Oettingen's mental contrasting), and each step as an
if-then on a real day (Gollwitzer's implementation intentions).

## What changes

- **A twenty-fourth view, `/act`.** An empty sheet starts from an example set from today; nothing is
  kept until _set it down_. Opened from a stone (`?id=`) or a kept envelope (`?botec=`), the
  intention is about that thing.
- **The steps**: when, then I will, where, for how long, on a day — dragged along the line to
  another day, the day meant kept. Each marked _done_ or _let go_, then what it was like (easier, as
  thought, harder) and whether what stands in the way showed up.
- **The drawing** sets meant above lived, a line between each step's two days, today marked.
- **The reading and the record**: what was meant, what was done and when, how long from setting it
  down to the first step, what the steps were like; across intentions, how many were acted on and
  how many steps done on the day meant. Counts, never a grade.
- **Act on it** on every stone (reader, catalogue page) and every kept envelope; the notice gets the
  act's card, step and key; the margin knows the view's name; the Mac app gets ⌘I.
- **The specimen.** Under `NIWA_MODE` the route serves Specimen A's intentions, read-only; PUT and
  DELETE 404.

## What it deliberately does not do

It reminds of nothing, sends nothing, keeps no streak, and never says what to do, whether a step was
enough, or whether it was kept to well. No model is asked; the steps are the reader's.
